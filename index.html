Self-Supervised

Frontier models are aligned by human preference labels: a rater reads two responses and picks the one they like better, and the model is trained to produce more of whatever gets picked.

Human approval is a bad proxy for good behavior. Optimizing against it is where sycophancy comes from — models learn to be agreeable, flattering, and deferential because that is what the signal rewards. The failure is not in the model; it is in the supervision.

We build the supervision. Evals and training environments for model character — sycophancy, honesty, epistemics — in which the grader reads the model’s internals as well as its transcripts. A model that says the right thing while representing something else should fail.

The research question is which internal signals can be trusted: which features survive being trained against, and which collapse under optimization pressure. Current focus is sycophancy and epistemic honesty. The long-term goal is character engineered as precisely as capability.
